# LinkedIn Newsletter

A professional feed where the newsletter competes for attention in a fast-moving stream before it reaches a subscriber's inbox. The 2026 algorithm optimizes for **professional relevance, dwell time, and comments** over raw reach — a focused piece reaching the right 5,000 beats a viral one reaching 500K randoms. Every piece should teach, prove, or humanize.

## Craft Rules

### Headline / hook (the most important element)
- The first two lines are everything — all the reader sees before the **"see more" fold**. Keep the hook **under ~210 characters**.
- Curiosity-driven openings win: a provocative question, a counterintuitive claim, a stakes-laden statement, or a statistic (numbers add credibility).
- Avoid generic AI-tell openers ("Leadership isn't about…"); they kill trust. Signal earned, insider insight.

### Structure
A repeatable, recognizable template builds loyalty:
1. **Hook** — 1 short paragraph (the pre-fold lines).
2. **Setup** — 1 paragraph framing problem + stakes.
3. **Framework / body** — ~3 paragraphs of teach/prove substance (a method, lessons, a breakdown).
4. **Personal story** — 1 paragraph that humanizes and proves it's lived.
5. **CTA** — 1 paragraph driving the next step.

Very short paragraphs (1-3 sentences), generous white space — it's read on a phone in-feed. Keep stakes clear throughout.

### Length
- Short-form editions: ~300-500 words, weekly.
- Long-form editions: ~1,500-2,000 words, bi-weekly or monthly.
Pick based on the user's cadence. Consistency beats frequency.

### Voice
Professional but human — real stories over theoretical fluff. The best issues share a specific challenge and how the writer solved it. Authority + authenticity. Tie the topic to a concrete professional outcome.

### Growth mechanic
**Dwell time + comments.** The algorithm rewards the "see more" click, time-on-post, and real comments. The hook earns the click; the framework holds attention; the CTA invites comments. Profile headline + Featured section should reinforce the same topic so engagement converts to follows/inbound. Best window: Tue-Thu mornings (~8-10 AM).

### Call to action
End with a question or prompt that invites comments. Make the next step obvious (subscribe, reply, related resource).

## Data Requirements

| Field | Required? | Limit / spec | Notes |
|-------|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Newsletter article title | Required | clear, specific, ≤~100 chars | shown in feed + email |
| Hook (first 2 lines) | Required | **≤210 chars before the fold** | validated separately — this is the make-or-break field |
| Subtitle/description | Optional | 1 line | some newsletter setups show a description |
| Cover image | Recommended | **1200×644** (LinkedIn article ratio) | with descriptive intent |
| Body | Required | per length target | short paragraphs |
| CTA | Required | comment-driving | a question works best for the algorithm |

### Notes
- Validate the pre-fold hook character count explicitly — it's the highest-leverage constraint and easy to overshoot.
- No formal tag field, but mention 3-5 relevant hashtags to append at the end (LinkedIn uses these for topic discovery).

## Publishing format

LinkedIn's newsletter editor is **rich-text**; raw markdown won't render. Set the **article title and cover image** in their fields; paste the body and format in the editor. The pre-fold hook must be the literal first two lines. Append the 3-5 hashtags at the end of the body. The metadata block is a what-to-set checklist.
