# Substack

Three things at once: email, blog, and a social feed (**Notes**). Success rests on the writer-reader relationship — strong writing is a *retention* engine; discovery comes from Notes, Recommendations, and cross-platform seeding (a large and growing share of new subscriptions — recently reported around 40% — originate inside the Substack network; verify the current figure in Stage 2 if you cite a number).

## Craft Rules

### Headline / hook
- The post title doubles as the **email subject line** — it decides opens. Front-load the value or curiosity; don't bury the hook.
- Specific, slightly longer, trust-building titles and questions often beat ultra-short punchy ones — they signal substance, not a tease.
- Open the body personally: a thought, confession, or scene, in the writer's real voice. This is what distinguishes Substack from a faceless content site.

### Structure
- One depth-focused idea, developed fully. Let the prose breathe — it's email + essay, not a scannable listicle by default.
- Short paragraphs; subheadings only as needed.
- A consistent personal voice matters more than rigid section formatting.

### Length
A substantial post, typically ~800-2,000+ words depending on the writer's rhythm. Consistency and depth matter more than a word count; the norm is one post per week.

### Voice
Personality must come through — readers subscribe because they like *how you think and write*. First-person, candid, behind-the-scenes where relevant. Write to one specific reader in a sharp niche, not "everyone."

### Growth mechanic
**Network surfaces.** Notes (the in-platform feed) is the primary discovery engine and where you're human, not promotional. Cross-platform seeding (tease the thinking on LinkedIn/X, link to the full essay) and Recommendation swaps with peers drive most network subscriptions. After the post, it's natural to offer a short **Notes teaser**.

### Call to action
Invite a reply or comment — Substack thrives on the two-way relationship. A clear, low-pressure subscribe prompt fits at the end. Build the free list first; paid conversion follows trust.

## Data Requirements

| Field | Required? | Limit / spec | Notes |
|-------|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Post title | Required | concise, specific | doubles as the email subject line |
| Subtitle / deck | Recommended | 1 line | shown under title and in previews |
| Cover image | Optional | landscape | improves the email + social card |
| Section/tags | Optional | per publication's sections | only if the user runs sections |
| SEO title | Optional | ≤60 chars | Substack lets you set social/SEO title separately |
| Social/meta description | Optional | ≤160 chars | for search + social card |

### Optional companion deliverable
A **Notes teaser** (1-3 short sentences) that surfaces one idea from the essay and links readers to the full piece — offer this after delivering the post.

## Publishing format

Substack uses a **rich-text editor**; raw markdown won't render. The title doubles as the email subject — set it in the title field. Paste the body and apply formatting in the editor. Set the **subtitle, social/SEO title and description** in Substack's post settings, not inline. The metadata block in the delivered file is a what-to-enter checklist.
