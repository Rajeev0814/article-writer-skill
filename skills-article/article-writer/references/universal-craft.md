# Universal craft rules

These hold on every platform. The platform spec layers its specifics on top; these are the floor.

- **Hook in the first 2-3 sentences.** State a problem, make a specific promise, or open a curiosity gap. Never warm up with throat-clearing ("In today's fast-paced world…"). The opening earns the next scroll or the next line.
- **One clear idea per piece.** A reader should be able to say in one sentence what it was about. Cut tangents.
- **Specific beats generic.** Concrete numbers, named examples, real stories, lived detail. "5 mistakes" beats "common mistakes"; a real anecdote beats a hypothetical. This is the single biggest lever for performance across all platforms.
- **Short paragraphs, scannable structure.** Most reading is on a phone. 2-4 sentences per paragraph. Use subheadings to break long stretches (each platform sets the cadence).
- **Deliver real value.** The reader should finish with something concrete — an insight, a method, a reframe, a story that lands. Surface-level listicles and recycled advice underperform everywhere.
- **End with purpose.** Every piece closes by pointing the reader somewhere — a reflection, a question, or a platform-appropriate CTA.
- **Sound human, not templated.** Vary sentence length. Avoid the tells of generic AI prose: every paragraph the same length, "Leadership isn't about…" openers, em-dash overuse, hollow transitions, "It's worth noting that." Write in the user's voice if samples are provided.
- **Earn the headline.** Whatever the title promises, the body must deliver. A great headline on a piece that doesn't pay it off costs trust.
